# Governance and Claim Policy

Voynich@Home combines public compute, contested interpretations, and a culturally
significant object. Transparent governance is part of the scientific method,
not an administrative afterthought.

## Stewardship bodies

Before public distribution, the project should charter:

- a **maintainer group** accountable for software, operations, releases, and the
  append-only experiment registry;
- a **scientific advisory group** with computational linguistics, historical
  cryptology, paleography/codicology, statistics, research software, and open-
  science expertise;
- a **security and volunteer group** with at least one elected volunteer
  representative and authority to halt unsafe workloads;
- independent **data stewards** who review provenance, attribution, cultural
  considerations, and redistribution terms;
- rotating **benchmark custodians** who can sequester known-answer material from
  implementers.

Membership, terms, recusals, conflicts, meeting records, and decision rationales
are public. No single proposer, sponsor, or infrastructure operator can approve
their own public workload.

## Experiment admission

A proposal receives compute only after checks for:

1. falsifiability and a declared nearest alternative;
2. appropriate controls and grouped evaluation;
3. complete data provenance and permitted processing;
4. bounded parameter space, cost, and stopping rule;
5. reviewed worker source and reproducible artifact;
6. volunteer safety, privacy, and energy proportionality;
7. a publication commitment covering negative outcomes;
8. conflicts and prior unpublished exploration.

Admission ranks scientific value per constrained joule, not publicity potential.
A public queue shows accepted, rejected, deferred, completed, and withdrawn
proposals with reasons. Security-sensitive details may be temporarily embargoed
under a documented disclosure deadline.

## Claim and communications rules

- Dashboards report registered findings and limitations, never “probability the
  manuscript is solved.”
- Press material uses the claim tier from the research protocol and links the
  complete result bundle.
- Volunteer credit reflects contributed validated work, not association with a
  claimed discovery.
- A candidate cannot be branded a translation when its mapping is incomplete,
  non-reversible, dependent on ad hoc glosses, or untested on confirmation data.
- Corrections and retractions remain linked to the original record.
- Project branding must not imply endorsement by Yale, the Beinecke Library,
  data providers, BOINC, or reviewers.

## Funding and public benefit

Sponsors, donated infrastructure, and in-kind work are disclosed. Sponsors do
not receive early results, private search capacity, data exclusivity, or claim
approval. Software should use an OSI-approved license and original documentation
an open-content license after the repository owner makes those legal choices;
third-party data retain their own terms.

No cryptocurrency, mining, token, non-fungible asset, speculative reward, or
sale of volunteer compute is permitted. The project may not silently repurpose
hosts for unrelated work, even when that work is charitable.

## Appeals and shutdown

Rejected proposers may appeal to a recused panel on procedural grounds. Security
and volunteer representatives may immediately pause distribution; resumption
requires documented remediation and release approval. If the project sunsets,
experiment metadata, source, findings, and reproduction bundles move to a
durable public archive, while host-linked operational data is deleted on the
published retention schedule.
