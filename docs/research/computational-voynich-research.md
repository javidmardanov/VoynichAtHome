# Computational Voynich Research: Findings for Workload Selection

*Web-research report compiled 2026-08-30 for the Voynich@Home proposal. Every load-bearing claim carries its source URL.*

## 1. The statistical fingerprint any model must explain

**Corpus size (why "big data" this is not):** ~170,000 glyphs, **37,919 word tokens, 8,114 word types**, ~20–30 core glyphs plus rare "weirdos" ([voynich.nu word statistics](https://voynich.nu/a4_word.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voynich_manuscript), [Adelaide statistical study](https://www.eleceng.adelaide.edu.au/personal/dabbott/wiki/images/8/82/Cracking_the_Voynich_Manuscript-_Using_basic_statistics_and_analyses_to_determine_linguistic_relationships.pdf)). The whole text fits in ~200 KB — every statistical test is cheap; compute demand comes only from the *number of hypotheses tested*.

**Conditional character entropy (h2):** The defining anomaly. Lindemann & Bowern, ["Character Entropy in Modern and Historical Texts"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14697) (also [PDF via Yale](https://alumniacademy.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2021-07/Character%20Entropy%20in%20Modern%20and%20Historical%20Texts%20Comparison%20Metrics%20for%20an%20Undeciphered%20Manuscript.pdf)): natural languages sit at **h2 ≈ 3–4 bits**; Voynichese **h2 ≈ 2.1** (Herbal-A, EVA), rising only to **≈ 2.4** under the Currier transcription — **lower than all 316 comparison texts** (294 Wikipedia languages + 18 historical texts + Voynich variants). Crucially, they found that "substantial manipulations of glyph composition are **not sufficient** to align conditional entropy with natural languages" — a direct, published challenge that a verbose-cipher parsing search (workload c below) could test exhaustively. h0/h1 (alphabet size, unigram frequencies) are unremarkable. Background: [voynich.nu entropy pages](https://www.voynich.nu/extra/sol_ent.html), [character statistics](https://www.voynich.nu/a2_char.html); caveat that EVA is stroke-based and depresses h2 (e.g. EVA `ch`): [Cipher Mysteries on transcription and parsing](https://ciphermysteries.com/2017/02/21/voynich-entropy-transcription-parsing).

**Zipf's laws:** Both Zipf's rank-frequency law and the length-frequency law hold ([Timm & Schinner](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01611194.2019.1596999); [fmjlang Zipf analysis](https://www.fmjlang.co.uk/voynich/VoynichZipf.html)) — necessary but weak evidence, since human gibberish also passes (see Gaskell & Bowern below).

**Word-length distribution:** Almost perfectly **binomial** (Stolfi), unusually narrow, unlike natural languages and unlike abjads ([Stolfi's statistical report](https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/links/programs/c/NEUROMAT/nmeeg_get_stats/projects/voynich/pendrive/stolfi-2012-05-11/more/stats/techrep/stat-report.pdf); [voynich.nu](https://voynich.nu/a4_word.html)). Both Schinner (2007) and Zandbergen (2021) showed generation mechanisms reproduce this as a side effect.

**Rigid word-internal morphology:** Stolfi's **crust–mantle–core** paradigm; modern sharpest form is Zattera's **12-slot "slot alphabet"** (HistoCrypt 2022): glyphs occupy ordered slots 0–11, transitions only to same-or-higher slots; **97% of word types conform** or decompose into conforming chunks; thousands of "**separable words**" split into two valid words ([Edwards' summary](https://medium.com/@robert.edwards_87384/voynich-reconsidered-the-glyphs-re-ordered-8e5905026bcc), [voynich.nu word structure](https://www.voynich.nu/a3_para.html)).

**Currier A/B and scribes:** Currier (1976) split the MS into "languages" A and B ([voynich.nu Currier extension](https://voynich.nu/extra/rz_lang.html)); Lisa Fagin Davis (2020) identified **five scribes** — scribes 1 & 4 write A, scribes 2, 3, 5 write B ([Manuscript Road Trip](https://manuscriptroadtrip.wordpress.com/category/voynich-manuscript/), [Edwards](https://medium.com/@robert.edwards_87384/voynich-reconsidered-scribes-and-languages-f9b1fd2a0996)). New quantitative confirmation: [arXiv 2604.25979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25979) fits a **Beta-Binomial mixture over 11 character-pair substitution ratios across 185 folios**; BIC selects K=2; **89% held-out folio label accuracy**; the distinction survives within-quire boundaries (not codicological); and A/B turns out to be a projection of a richer system whose dominant component is a per-folio boolean "switch" governing the vowel after `ch`/`sh`, with word-template identity explaining **92% of variance**.

**Line as functional unit (LAAFU):** Currier's observation; line-initial words are longer, second words shorter; certain glyphs prefer line-start (68% vs 50% baseline); EVA `m` is **62% line-final** ([Elmar Vogt's "The Line as a Functional Unit" PDF](https://voynichthoughts.files.wordpress.com/2012/11/the_voynich_line.pdf), [Agnostic Voynich](https://agnosticvoynich.wordpress.com/2015/03/29/linefirst-words/)). Steckley & Steckley ([arXiv 2404.13069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13069), HistoCrypt 2024) extend this: token distributions shift around *extrinsic* boundaries too — i.e., the plant drawings.

**Low word mobility / burstiness / self-similarity:** Similar-spelled tokens cluster in close vicinity (Timm, ["How the Voynich Manuscript was created"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.6639)); Schinner's random-walk analysis found token-repetition distance statistics and long-range correlations more consistent with a stochastic generation process than language ([Cryptologia 31(2) 2007](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611190601133539)). Counterpoint: Montemurro & Zanette found language-like long-range information clustering and semantic co-occurrence networks ([PLOS ONE 2013](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0066344)), itself rebutted by Timm ([arXiv 1601.07435](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.07435)).

**Newest constraint set (the benchmark to beat):** Parisel, [arXiv 2604.19762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19762) (2026): a **right-to-left optimization inside words vs a left-to-right dependency at word boundaries** (a "directional dissociation" absent from English, French, Hebrew, Arabic); **97% of cross-boundary mutual information sits in specific within-class grapheme transitions**; **80.6% end-class→start-class transition rate**; Zipfian boundary distributions. He proposes a **four-signature joint criterion** as an explicit benchmark for future generative/cryptanalytic models. See also his earlier [Directionality of the Voynich Script, arXiv 2509.10573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10573).

## 2. "Meaningless text" generators in computational form

- **Rugg 2004, table + Cardan grille** ([Cryptologia 28(1)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/0161-110491892755); [An Elegant Hoax?](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233458814_An_Elegant_Hoax_A_Possible_Solution_to_the_Voynich_Manuscript)): words assembled from a table of prefix/midfix/suffix fragments through a moving 3-hole grille. Parameters: table contents and dimensions, hole geometry, movement schedule. Never widely accepted ([Cipher Mysteries critique](https://ciphermysteries.com/2016/09/14/gordon-rugg-man-cracked-mystery-voynich-manuscript-cracks-not); [Rugg's reply](https://hydeandrugg.wordpress.com/2016/09/25/the-rugg-and-taylor-cryptologia-article-on-the-voynich-manuscript/)).
- **Zandbergen 2021** took the grille seriously and computationally: ["The Cardan grille approach... taken to the next level"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12548) ([PDF at voynich.nu](https://www.voynich.nu/papers/Grilles_RZ_2021.pdf)) — a simpler, more generic fragment-table method reproducing the **binomial word-length distribution**.
- **Schinner 2007** ([Evidence of the Hoax Hypothesis, Cryptologia 31(2):95–107](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1080/01611190601133539)): random-walk mapping + token/syllable repetition statistics → text consistent with a quasi-stochastic (Rugg-like) process.
- **Timm & Schinner 2020, self-citation** ([Cryptologia 44(1), DOI 10.1080/01611194.2019.1596999](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01611194.2019.1596999); precursor [arXiv 1407.6639](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.6639)): scribe copies and mutates earlier tokens ("self-citation"). **Open-source generator**: [TorstenTimm/SelfCitationTextgenerator](https://github.com/TorstenTimm/SelfCitationTextgenerator) (archived at [Zenodo 2531632](https://zenodo.org/record/2531632)); mutation operators are `tryTo...()` functions (rule-guided glyph substitutions/affix changes) applied to a source word chosen from recently written text. **Goodness of fit: point comparisons of word-length distribution, entropy values, Zipf laws, repetition statistics, achieving <10% relative error** — i.e., *no formal likelihood, no ABC, no sweep*. Follow-up debate: [Timm & Schinner 2023, Cryptologia 48(4)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01611194.2023.2225716).
- **Human gibberish baseline:** Gaskell & Bowern 2022, ["Gibberish after all?"](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/paper4.pdf) — 42 volunteers writing gibberish reproduce either natural-language or Voynichese statistics depending on the writer; data/tools at [danielgaskell/voynich](https://github.com/danielgaskell/voynich).
- **Naibbe cipher (the new cipher-side generator), Greshko 2025/2026** ([Cryptologia, DOI 10.1080/01611194.2025.2566408](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01611194.2025.2566408); coverage: [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/archaeology/mysterious-voynich-manuscript-may-be-a-cipher-a-new-study-suggests), [Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/othersciences/linguistics/voynich-manuscript-cipher-14466.html), [Schneier](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2025/12/substitution-cipher-based-on-the-voynich-manuscript.html)): a **verbose homophonic substitution cipher** — plaintext (Latin/Italian) split into 1–2-letter chunks by dice, each chunk encoded as a Voynichese "word" via one of **six substitution tables selected by playing-card draws**; fully hand-executable with 15th-century materials, **fully decipherable**, and reproduces glyph frequencies, word structure, entropy levels, and even absolute frequencies of Voynich B word types. Code: [greshko/naibbe-cipher](https://github.com/greshko/naibbe-cipher) (includes "Voynichesque" control generator); data: [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.16415087](https://zenodo.org/records/17794596).
- **Has anyone swept?** Only just, and only small: [Parisel 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19762) tested a parametric slot-based generator and a Rugg grille against his four-signature criterion — **"neither class reproduces all four signatures simultaneously across their full tested parameter spaces."** A targeted search for ABC (approximate Bayesian computation) applied to Voynich generation models found **nothing** — no published ABC or large-scale model comparison across generator families exists ([ABC methodology exists off-the-shelf](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1002803)). This is a genuine, freshly-benchmarked gap.

## 3. Computational decipherment attempts

- **Reddy & Knight 2011**, ["What We Know About the Voynich Manuscript"](https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511/): bigram predictability closer to Pinyin than English/Arabic; word-length and letter-entropy profiles unlike any natural language *or* known cipher; abjad-like possibilities discussed; unsupervised (HMM-style) analyses fail to yield the clean vowel/consonant partition natural scripts give.
- **Knight's Copiale success 2011** ([Knight, Megyesi & Schaefer](https://mt-archive.net/BUCC-2011-Knight-2.pdf); [USC announcement](https://today.usc.edu/usc-scientist-cracks-mysterious-copiale-cipher/)): EM/Bayesian noisy-channel decipherment + language ID against 80 candidate languages cracked a homophonic German cipher — the proof that the pipeline works *when there is plaintext to find*. Method survey: [Knight's ACL decipherment tutorial](https://kevincrawfordknight.github.io/extra/acl-tutorial-13-decipher-final.pdf); neural successor: [Can Sequence-to-Sequence Models Crack Substitution Ciphers?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.15229).
- **Hauer & Kondrak 2016** ([TACL Q16-1006](https://aclanthology.org/Q16-1006/)): source-language ID 97% over 380 languages; anagram-decryption 93% average word accuracy on 50 test ciphertexts; applied to Voynich, **Hebrew ranks first** — but the authors themselves flag the result may be "artifacts of the combinatorial power of anagramming and language models," and the community rejected the decipherment ([Zandbergen's critique](http://www.voynich.nu/misc/misc_01.html), [Cipher Mysteries](https://ciphermysteries.com/2018/01/27/kondrak-hauer-finally-revealed-voynich-manuscripts-secrets-errrm-no-not-really-sorry), [Artnet on the 2018 AI hype](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/ai-decode-voynich-manuscript-1241105)). This is the canonical cautionary tale for any decipherment-search workload.
- **Recent (2024–2026):** [arXiv 2404.13069](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13069v1) (scribal intent, 2024); [arXiv 2509.10573](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10573) (directionality, 2025); [arXiv 2604.19762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19762) and [arXiv 2604.25979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25979) (both 2026, detailed above — the latter is exactly the "unsupervised generative model recovers Currier's split blind" result). Community tooling has gone cryptanalytic: [seismicgear/voynich-mcmc-annealer](https://github.com/seismicgear/voynich-mcmc-annealer) (positional polyalphabetic decoder via simulated annealing, BPE tokenization, trigram cosine similarity vs 15th-century corpora) and [Krymorn/The-Voynich-Transliteration-Tool](https://github.com/Krymorn/The-Voynich-Transliteration-Tool) (build/test substitution mappings statistically, with a solver). No serious peer-reviewed LLM-based decipherment exists; LLM involvement so far is mostly meta-commentary. There is also an independent distributed-verification research pipeline, [brigadire/voinich](https://github.com/brigadire/voinich) (Rozanova & Temerev 2026; companion repo [lrozanova/voynich-units](https://github.com/lrozanova/voynich-units) — "Neither simple substitution nor stable word segmentation," with control corpora and per-word image coordinates), which uses ZL3b as canonical input with frozen manifests and content-addressed work units — evidence that rigorous distributed Voynich work is already considered credible.

## 4. Data: transliterations, formats, licenses

- **Hub:** [voynich.nu/transcr.html](https://www.voynich.nu/transcr.html) (main transliteration page) and the data directory `voynich.nu/data/`.
- **IVTFF format:** ["Intermediate Voynich MS Transliteration File Format"](http://www.voynich.nu/software/ivtt/IVTFF_format.pdf), now ~v2.x. Header `#=IVTFF Eva- 2.0`; page headers like `<f1r> <! $Q=A $P=A $I=T $L=A $H=1 $C=1>` carry per-page variables — quire ($Q), illustration type ($I), **Currier language ($L)**, **hand/scribe ($H)** — so Currier A/B and Davis's five scribes are machine-readable page metadata. Locus types distinguish paragraphs, labels, circular text. Companion tool: [IVTT](https://www.voynich.nu/software/ivtt/IVTT_manual.pdf), already **compiled to WebAssembly** at [jere-mie/ivtt-wasm](https://github.com/jere-mie/ivtt-wasm); Python parser: [jere-mie/ivtff-py](https://github.com/jere-mie/ivtff-py).
- **Key files** (names verified from downstream tooling): **ZL_ivtff_*.txt** — Zandbergen–Landini "ZL" reference transliteration, current generation **ZL3b** (older cited name `ZL_ivtff_2b.txt`); **`GC2a-n.txt`** — Glen Claston's **v101** alphabet in IVTFF (finer glyph distinctions than EVA), at [voynich.nu/data/GC2a-n.txt](https://www.voynich.nu/data/GC2a-n.txt); **`LSI_ivtff_0d.txt`** — Landini–Stolfi Interlinear at [voynich.nu/data/beta/LSI_ivtff_0d.txt](http://www.voynich.nu/data/beta/LSI_ivtff_0d.txt); **IT_ivtff** — Takeshi Takahashi's 1998–99 transcription (first complete one, full EVA), original in [Stolfi's interlinear archive 16e6](https://www.ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/EXPORT/00-EXPORT/98-12-28-interln16e6/) (Takahashi = tag "H", e.g. [source-H.evt](https://ic.unicamp.br/~stolfi/voynich/Notes/105/source-H.evt)). Zandbergen's own overview: [CEUR keynote](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3313/keynote1.pdf).
- **Mirrors / packaged datasets:** [Krymorn/The-Voynich-Transliteration-Tool](https://github.com/Krymorn/The-Voynich-Transliteration-Tool) bundles **nine** voynich.nu transcriptions with SHA checksums; Hugging Face [AncientLanguages/Voynich](https://huggingface.co/datasets/AncientLanguages/Voynich) and [Ched-ai/voynich-eva](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Ched-ai/voynich-eva/viewer); [Zenodo "Voynich Manuscript – Full Dataset v2.0"](https://zenodo.org/records/18215102); positioned-token spatial data (incl. the Nine Rosettes foldout) under **CC BY 4.0** at [alessandroplaca-uro/voynich-spatial-data](https://github.com/alessandroplaca-uro/voynich-spatial-data).
- **Licensing:** the manuscript itself is public domain (Beinecke MS 408). The voynich.nu transliterations carry **no formal OSI/CC license** — they are "freely published for research" with attribution to Zandbergen, Landini, Takahashi et al. requested. For a public volunteer project that *redistributes* the data to every client, get explicit permission from René Zandbergen (he is approachable and active), or fall back to the CC BY 4.0 spatial dataset / Stolfi archive.

## 5. Honest assessment: compute-bound vs insight-bound

The blunt truth first: **finding the plaintext is insight-bound, not compute-bound.** The corpus is 38k tokens; if it is a cipher it is not a simple substitution (h2 too low, morphology too rigid), and unknown language + unknown segmentation + possible meaninglessness means brute force has no well-defined target. What IS compute-bound is **hypothesis falsification at scale** — the field's active frontier (Parisel's benchmarks, Greshko's cipher, Timm's generator) consists of hand-tuned point demonstrations that nobody has swept.

**(a) Generator parameter sweeps / ABC model comparison — genuinely compute-bound.** Simulating one candidate corpus (38k tokens) plus computing a ~20-statistic fingerprint (h2, word-length distribution, Zipf slopes, slot-grammar conformance, repetition-distance profile, LAAFU effects, Parisel's four signatures, A/B switch behavior) takes **seconds**. The joint space — {self-citation, Rugg/Zandbergen grille, Naibbe, slot generators, human-gibberish-inspired Markov} × parameter vectors × stochastic replicates × per-section fits (A vs B, five scribes) — is astronomically large, and ABC-style posterior model comparison *requires* millions of simulations. Work unit: `(model family, θ, seed) → summary-statistics vector`, ~1 KB result, perfectly deterministic given seed (validation by replication). Nobody has done it; Parisel 2026 explicitly frames his signatures as the benchmark for exactly this.

**(b) Noisy-channel decipherment grid (cipher family × language × segmentation × restarts) — compute-hungry but scientifically dangerous.** Each cell is an EM/annealing run (minutes–hours); embarrassingly parallel; could absorb millions of CPU-hours. But the Hauer–Kondrak episode shows the failure mode: powerful search + language models *always* "decode" something. Defensible only in the negative-control framing: run every cell on the real MS **and** on matched synthetic controls (self-citation output, Naibbe output, shuffled MS), and report whether the MS decodes *better than gibberish* anywhere — a "decipherment susceptibility map." Existing single-machine prototypes: [voynich-mcmc-annealer](https://github.com/seismicgear/voynich-mcmc-annealer), the [genetic-algorithm cipher-wheels blog](https://voynichattacks.wordpress.com/2021/08/06/cipher-wheels-genetic-algorithm/).

**(c) Verbose-cipher glyph-grouping search — the crispest falsifiable compute-bound question.** Lindemann & Bowern claim no glyph-composition manipulation reaches natural h2; forum practitioners using greedy n-gram merging claim they got there ([voynich.ninja entropy threads](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-148-2.html), [Entropy Hunting series](https://herculeaf.wordpress.com/2020/10/19/entropy-hunting-bigger-and-better/), [verbose cipher thread](https://www.voynich.ninja/archive/index.php/thread-3356-3.html)). The space of merge/split parsings of EVA/v101 is combinatorially huge; greedy search proves nothing about global optima. A distributed search (beam/GA/annealing islands over parsings, scored on h2-normalization subject to h0/h1 and word-structure sanity constraints, with natural-language and gibberish controls) would settle a genuinely debated question and directly stress-test Naibbe-class verbose structures. Work units are tiny — each candidate parsing evaluation is O(text length), milliseconds.

**(d) Community wishlist:** voynich.ninja's computational threads ([edit-distance attack](https://www.voynich.ninja/thread-655.html), entropy/verbose-cipher threads) and solo projects above show people already run single-machine versions of (b) and (c) and hit compute walls at the "many restarts × many hypotheses" stage. No volunteer-computing project for the Voynich exists — Voynich@Home would be the first.

## Ranked recommendation for the first workload

**1. "The Voynich Fingerprint" — ABC-style parameter sweep and Bayesian model comparison of text generators (workload a). Recommended first.**
- *Scientific defensibility: high.* It extends, at scale, exactly what Cryptologia/arXiv published in 2020–2026 (Timm & Schinner's <10%-error point fit; Zandbergen's grille; Greshko's Naibbe; Parisel's four-signature sweep that no generator passed). Pre-register the fingerprint statistics, include natural-language and human-gibberish controls, and the output — a posterior ranking of generation mechanisms, or the finding that *no* parameterization of any proposed mechanism matches the full fingerprint — is publishable either way. It cannot produce an embarrassing false "solution."
- *Parallelism: perfect.* Independent (model, θ, seed) triples; no inter-unit communication; deterministic replication for validation.
- *Work-unit size: ideal for a browser tab.* Generate ~38k tokens + ~20 statistics = seconds per unit in WASM; the reference target ships once (~KBs); results are ~1 KB. Open-source seeds exist for porting: [SelfCitationTextgenerator](https://github.com/TorstenTimm/SelfCitationTextgenerator), [naibbe-cipher](https://github.com/greshko/naibbe-cipher), and [ivtt-wasm](https://github.com/jere-mie/ivtt-wasm) already proves Voynich tooling runs in-browser.
- *Public appeal: strong.* "Your browser just wrote a page of fake Voynich and tested whether a medieval scribe could have made the real one" — with live rendering of the volunteer's generated Voynichese as the hook.

**2. Verbose-cipher mapping search (workload c) — second workload.** Equally browser-sized units and a sharper yes/no question ("does any glyph parsing normalize Voynichese entropy?"), but the negative result needs careful search design (coverage guarantees over a combinatorial space) to be airtight, and it is harder to explain to volunteers. Natural follow-on sharing the same fingerprint infrastructure.

**3. Decipherment susceptibility grid (workload b) — third, once credibility exists.** The most compute-hungry and most headline-friendly, but highest reputational risk; requires the null-control framing and mature infrastructure to avoid becoming the next "AI deciphered the Voynich" debacle.

**Practical flags:** confirm redistribution terms for ZL3b with Zandbergen (no formal license); use IVTFF page variables ($L, $H) to fit per-language/per-scribe from day one; every generated corpus should be scored against Currier A and B separately since the 2026 mixture-model paper shows a single global fingerprint is already known to be too coarse.
